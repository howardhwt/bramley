# Design: The agent pager, Bramley as a calm pocket device

## Problem Statement

Howard wants Bramley to be "the apple/ ios of the Trimui Brick community": a curated, personal, clean experience that unlocks what the Brick's hardware can do. Most owners treat the Brick as a game console; Howard sees it as "a personal EDC device which just so happens to play games", and in the north star it should "run agents or control cloud claude/ other llm agents in customized pak or app".

His day, in his words: "on the train I check my agents while playing music, and once the agents direction are approved and running I either continue to play music or play some games or stream videos on the brick."

## What Makes This Cool

- The phone is the worst place to supervise agents: it is the device that pulls you into scrolling, and an approval is a one-bit decision. A calm device with a rumble motor, an A button and a B button is a better agent pager than an iPhone, and no handheld firmware does this.
- The moment that matters is the handoff: one decisive approval, then back to music or a game. The pager is built for that moment, not for watching agents.

## Constraints

- Hardware: Allwinner A133P (4x Cortex-A53), 1 GB RAM, 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi only, rumble motor, D-pad, ABXY, four shoulder buttons, no touch, no analog stick. It cannot run a useful language model.
- On the train the Brick reaches the internet only through a phone hotspot; nobody has measured how long its Wi-Fi stays connected and what that costs in battery.
- Bramley is free and never sold (PolyForm Noncommercial). Wi-Fi drivers and services and anything that touches saves need Howard's OK (CLAUDE.md).
- No new visual language; the in-game hold-to-allow bar belongs to Approach B.
- The pager reuses Bramley's existing pieces: the launcher's GL kit glass cards and button badges (as in Settings), Inter, the single accent green #4AB278, the spring slide-in motion, `notification.c`'s HUD and toast drawing over games, and the in-game menu's look (`bramley_menu.c`). Home and Now Playing stay exactly as they are under the card. New in v1: the request card's content, the Y details view, the in-game toast and the finished toast.

## Premises

1. Bramley's identity shifts from a game console OS to a calm pocket device that plays games beautifully; agents, music and games are peers on Home. This is the north star, delivered through Approach B (agent tiles, Activity); v1 adds only the pager.
2. The first agent feature is oversight, not running models: the Brick watches and approves agents that run elsewhere (Claude Code on the Mac or in the cloud).
3. It is built on Claude Code's own hooks and a small open message format, so other agents (Codex, scripts) can use the same Brick.
4. Security from day one: every message is encrypted and signed with a shared key, so only holders of the key can reach the Brick or read a request. In v1 the key is set up by hand (a file on each side); real pairing by QR or short code comes with Approach B.
5. Revised after the second opinion: on Home and in Now Playing a request arrives as a banner and a buzz and is answered with one deliberate press (A allows, B denies), guarded against presses meant for something else. During a game it never takes input: v1 shows a toast only; Approach B adds pausing the game and a deliberate hold (L2 + R2 + A) while the full command shows. Music never stops.
6. Video streaming stays out of this design until it is measured on the hardware.

## Cross-Model Perspective

An independent Claude subagent read a summary of the session (Codex was not installed).

- Coolest version: agent sessions as cartridges. Running agents get tiles in Continue Playing with a progress ring and their last action; Activity counts "agent time" next to play time; an approval during a game takes a save state, slides the card over the frozen frame, and resumes on the same frame; each agent has its own rumble pattern.
- What excites Howard most: "once the agents direction are approved and running I either continue to play music or play some games". The handoff, not the watching.
- Halfway there: ntfy (publish and subscribe over HTTP, a stream the device can follow, self-hostable) plus Claude Code's PermissionRequest and Notification hooks, already joined for phones by short scripts. Bramley builds the blocking hook with a timeout, the Brick listener and buzz, the banner and approval UI, two-line summaries, and signing.
- Weekend build: a Python hook on the Mac posts a signed request to a random ntfy topic and waits up to 90 seconds on a reply topic, falling back to the terminal prompt; a libcurl listener on the Brick checks the signature, writes the request, buzzes; the launcher shows it on Home and Now Playing. Skip pairing UI, in-game, other agents and history at first. Measure Wi-Fi battery drain on a hotspot first.
- Challenged premise 5 (High): A and B are game inputs, so a press meant for the game could approve a destructive command. Howard revised the premise.

Where this design differs: ntfy's public server is fine for the prototype, but an approval channel should move to a self-hosted relay before any other user relies on it; and a key file on the card is a weekend shortcut, not a replacement for pairing (premise 4).

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: The pager on Home (chosen)
The smallest loop that proves the train moment: hook, relay, Brick listener, banner and buzz, one press. Effort M, a long weekend.

### Approach B: Agents as a first-class part of Bramley
Open protocol, self-hosted relay, QR pairing, adapters for several agents, agent tiles, Activity agent time, the in-game pause flow, rumble patterns. Effort XL. Not first: weeks of building before knowing the Brick's Wi-Fi survives a commute. A grows into B.

### Approach C: An agent inbox
No persistent connection; the Brick checks in on wake and every few minutes and approvals are batched. Kept as the fallback if the battery test fails.

## Recommended Approach

Approach A, built with B's message format so every piece carries forward.

### Gate before any code: the hook contract
Verify in the current Claude Code what a PermissionRequest hook receives (and when the Stop hook fires and what it receives; whether the terminal prompt stays usable while a hook waits, which decides OV-1, the Brick presence heartbeat; and, in 30 minutes, what Claude Code's Remote Control and ntfy's own phone app already do for approvals, recorded here without changing direction, OV-3), how it returns allow or deny, its maximum timeout (set it explicitly above 90 seconds in the hook's settings), and the exact "no decision" output that hands control back to the normal terminal prompt. If a hook cannot block that long or cannot defer to the prompt, the Mac side changes before anything else is built.

### Messages
- Requests, cancels and finished notices travel on a request topic; answers travel on a separate answer topic. Both topic names are derived from the key. Every envelope carries `type` (`request`, `answer`, `cancel`, `finished`, `receipt`); receipts travel on the request topic.
- Every message is a JSON envelope encrypted and authenticated with the shared key (XChaCha20-Poly1305: Monocypher vendored as one C file on the Brick, public domain or BSD-2, listed in THIRD-PARTY-LICENSES.md; PyNaCl on the Mac), so the relay and anyone who learns the topic see only ciphertext. Commands can hold secrets and private names; nothing travels in the clear.
- A request carries `{v, id (random 128-bit), agent, project, tool, command, summary, ttl}`, where `ttl` is 10 seconds shorter than the hook's own wait (the hook waits 90, sends 80), so a card always leaves the Brick before the hook stops listening. The Brick never compares wall clocks, since its network time is a user setting and can be off: a card lives for `ttl` seconds from when the daemon received it, by the Brick's own `CLOCK_BOOTTIME` clock, which keeps counting through suspend (eng review, outside voice #6). Replays are blocked by id: the daemon shows a request only if it decrypts and its `id` is not among the last 1024 seen (kept in `/tmp/bramley-pager/seen`, so a daemon restart does not forget them; a reboot clears it, which is safe because a request is only live while its hook waits). A replayed old request that slips past that still cannot approve anything, because the hook accepts answers only for the one id it is waiting on. (Revised by eng D8.) On reconnect the daemon asks the relay for messages since the last one it saw (`since=<last message id>`). A request is shown only if the newest relay `time` the daemon has seen, minus the request's own relay `time`, is under its ttl, and its card lives only for the remainder; a cancel in the catch-up removes its request first. The relay's clock judges age, so the Brick's clock is never trusted and a stale request can never come back with a fresh ttl.
- An answer carries `{v, type, id, decision}`, encrypted the same way. The hook accepts only an answer that decrypts, matches the pending `id`, and arrives before its own timer runs out; anything else is ignored.
- When the hook times out it sends `{v, type: cancel, id}`; the Brick removes that card. If the gate shows a hook can also see the terminal prompt being answered, it sends the same cancel then. A late answer the hook no longer waits for is discarded.

### The key
- v1 key setup is by hand: one command on the Mac (`agent-pager keygen`) writes the key to `~/.config/bramley-pager/key` (mode 0600) and prints the path of a copy for the card, `.userdata/shared/.bramley/pager/key`. The card is FAT, so anyone holding the SD card can read the key; if the card is lost, run keygen again on the Mac, which revokes the old key. Only the Brick's listener reads it. Rotating means running keygen again and copying the new file; deleting the file on either side revokes.
- The ntfy topic name is derived from the key, so it carries no meaning on its own.

### Process model on the Brick
- A small daemon, `bramley-pager`, separate from the launcher. The launcher starts it at startup when the pager is switched on in Settings (unlike Muse, which starts lazily, lmuse.c:131-147), detached with a setsid fork the way the launcher spawns the Muse daemon (`spawn_daemon`, `lmuse.c:98-142`), so it outlives launcher restarts, game launches and the crash fallback to the stock launcher. It takes an exclusive `flock` on `/tmp/bramley-pager/pid` and exits at once if another copy holds it, so two daemons never run; turning the switch off in Settings sends it SIGTERM at once, and turning it on spawns it at once.
- It alone holds the key, follows the relay's live stream by running the `curl` command with `-N` (no output buffering, so each message is read the moment it arrives), no speed limit and no total time limit, and with Bramley's CA bundle (`.system/res/bramley/cacert.pem` on the card, from `workspace/all/launcher/res/cacert.pem`), exactly as Software Update runs curl (`bramley_update.c:271`), and restarts that curl when the stream drops. It decrypts and checks requests, writes each pending request to `/tmp/bramley-pager/pending/<id>.json`, and leaves the rumble to the foreground program: the launcher or minarch, which already run Bramley's vibration API (`VIB_doublePulse` for requests, `VIB_singlePulse` for finished notices, api.h:558-559), buzzes when it sees a new file that has no `<id>.buzzed` marker, then creates the marker, so a request buzzes once however many programs see it. The daemon itself drives no hardware.
- The launcher watches that folder and shows cards; an answer is written to `/tmp/bramley-pager/answers/<id>`, which the daemon encrypts and posts. The launcher never touches the key or the network for this.
- Under the stock fallback launcher nothing shows a card; requests expire and the Mac falls back to its own prompt.

### On the Brick's screen
- Home and Now Playing: Bramley's existing glass card and button badges, as in the approved wireframe, state 1 (`~/.gstack/projects/howardhwt-bramley/howard-main-sketch-20261104-212445.png`). Y opens the full request. Music never stops.
- Input guard: while a card is shown it takes all input. A or B counts only when pressed and released after the card has finished sliding in plus 300 ms; a button already held when the card appears is ignored until released.
- Several requests: cards queue oldest first; the card shows "1 of 3", L1 and R1 move between them, and answering one shows the next. An expired or cancelled card leaves with a short "Answered on your Mac" or "Timed out" note.
- During a game (v1): the daemon's buzz plus a non-interactive toast drawn by minarch through `notification.c` ("Claude Code needs you: answer from Home"). minarch checks `/tmp/bramley-pager/pending/` and `/tmp/bramley-pager/notices/` every 500 ms (a directory listing, no inotify) on a background thread, never on the frame loop, as the launcher does. Other Paks get nothing in v1; the request waits on Home until it expires. A and B are never read for the pager during a game. The request stays queued until it expires; leaving the game shows it if it is still pending.
- If the rumble does not fire on some unit, the card and the toast still appear; the buzz is an addition, not the only signal.

### Finished notices (added in the CEO review, EXP-1)
- "Finished" means a Claude Code turn ended after running at least 60 seconds; quick back-and-forth turns never notify, and one project notifies at most once every 2 minutes. Claude Code's Stop hook fires at the end of every turn, so the hook script applies these rules before posting.
- The hook posts `{v, type: finished, id, agent, project, summary}` on the request topic and does not wait.
- The daemon writes it to `/tmp/bramley-pager/notices/<id>.json`; the launcher or minarch shows a read-only toast on any screen ("bramley-film: finished") with one short pulse, then deletes the file. Notices older than 5 minutes are deleted unshown.

### Not in v1
The in-game pause and hold-to-allow (wireframe state 2; pausing on a save state needs Howard's approval because it touches save states, so B uses minarch's in-memory pause unless he approves), pairing UI, other agents, history, agent tiles.

## Open Questions

1. How long does the Brick's Wi-Fi stay connected on a phone hotspot, and what does it cost in battery? Pass line: no more than 20 percentage points of battery above the same 2-hour ride with Wi-Fi off, and no drop that lasts longer than a minute. Fail means Approach C.
2. Does a held-open connection survive the Brick's screen-off sleep, or does the pager only work while the screen is on? Bramley's lock screen and Wi-Fi power saving interact here, and changing Wi-Fi services needs Howard's OK.
3. Does Claude Code's Remote Control already cover part of this, and could Bramley use it instead of hooks later? (The hook contract itself is the gate above.)

## Success Criteria

- On a phone hotspot, a Claude Code permission request on the Mac reaches the Brick in under 5 seconds, shows a card (and buzzes, where the motor works), and an A press releases the Mac's prompt.
- During a game, a request shows only a toast and no button press in the game ever answers it.
- A press already held, or pressed while the card slides in, never answers it.
- Two requests at once both show, in order, and each answer reaches the right session.
- When a Claude Code session finishes, a "finished" toast shows within 5 seconds on Home, Now Playing or over a game.
- If the Brick does not answer in 90 seconds, the Mac falls back to its own prompt; nothing hangs.
- Music keeps playing through the whole exchange.
- A message that fails to decrypt, repeats an id, or is older than its ttl by the relay's clock never shows a card; a reply for a different or expired request is ignored by the hook.
- A request that arrives while the Brick is locked buzzes within 5 s (eng D5).
- A never allows a command that was not fully on screen: longer than 2 lines needs the Y view first, and a cut request cannot be allowed on the Brick (eng D7).
- At the Mac (used in the last 60 s), permission prompts behave exactly as without the pager (eng D6).
- The relay operator never sees a command in the clear.
- The Brick lasts a 2-hour commute with the pager on, within the pass line in Open Question 1.

## Review additions (CEO review, Sections 1 to 4)

### Spool file ownership (Section 1, clarification)
- The daemon creates `pending/<id>.json` and `notices/<id>.json` by writing a temporary name and renaming it, so readers never see a half-written file.
- The launcher deletes `pending/<id>.json` when it writes the answer; the daemon deletes it on cancel or expiry. minarch only reads. The launcher or minarch deletes a notice after showing it; the daemon deletes notices older than 5 minutes when it writes a new one.

### Error rescue (ERR-1, approved D9: the full rescue set)
- Hook: if posting the request fails, the key is missing or unreadable, or the hook input is malformed, the hook returns "no decision" at once and Claude Code shows its own terminal prompt; the reason goes to the hook's log.
- Daemon: on stream loss or a TLS/CA error it logs the error and restarts curl with backoff (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 30 s, then every 30 s); it refuses to start without a key, and Settings shows "Key missing" under the pager switch. A malformed envelope is dropped and logged.
- A failed answer post is retried until the request's `ttl` ends; then the card shows "Not sent. Answer on your Mac".
- Spool writes that fail are logged and retried once.

### Decision record (SEC-1, approved D10: both sides)
- The hook appends one line per decision to `~/.config/bramley-pager/decisions.log` (mode 0600, rotated at 1 MB): time, project, tool, command, allow / deny / timeout / fallback, and whether the answer came from the Brick or the Mac.
- The Brick keeps its last 200 decisions in `/tmp/bramley-pager/decisions.log` (lost on reboot; no SD card writes).

### Text and flood limits (SEC-2, approved D11)
- (Sizes revised by D15, OV-2: ntfy refuses or converts messages over 4 KB.) The hook caps a request's plaintext at 2.5 KB before encrypting, cutting the command with a visible "(cut, full text on your Mac)" note. The daemon drops envelopes over 4 KB before decrypting, and keeps at most 20 pending requests (the oldest are kept; extras are dropped and logged).
- Control characters are stripped from every text field. The card shows the command in at most 2 lines; the Y view shows the full request as it arrived.
- The summary is shown as the agent's own words: "Claude says: ...".

### Answer receipts (ORD-1, approved D12)
- When the hook applies an answer it posts `{v, type: receipt, id, decision}`. The card shows "Allowed" or "Denied" only after the receipt arrives, and "Not confirmed. Check your Mac" if none arrives within 10 seconds.

### Connection status (OBS-1, approved D13)
- The daemon keeps `/tmp/bramley-pager/status` current: connected (with the time of the last message), reconnecting (with the reason, such as no Wi-Fi), or key missing.
- Settings shows it as one line under the pager switch, in the existing row-description style: "Connected · last message 2 min ago", "Reconnecting (no Wi-Fi)", "Key missing", and "Can't connect: check the clock" when TLS fails on certificate dates.

### Logs and rollback (Sections 8 and 9, clarifications)
- The daemon logs to `.userdata/tg5040/logs/bramley-pager.txt`, beside `bramley.txt`, rotated at 256 KB: start, connect, disconnect with reason, each message's id and type, drops with reason, answers posted, receipts. Command text is never written to the card.
- The Mac side gets `agent-pager uninstall`, which removes its hooks from Claude Code's settings, so the Mac side rolls back in one command.

### Interaction details (Section 4, clarifications)
- If a request's `ttl` ends while its Y view is open, the Y view closes with the card and its "Timed out" note.
- If the power button locks the Brick while a card is up, the request stays pending and shows again on unlock while its `ttl` lasts.
- A finished notice that arrives while a card is up waits until the card closes.

### Error & Rescue Registry
| Codepath | Failure | Error class | Rescued | Action | User sees |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| hook post | relay unreachable | RelayPostFailed | Y | instant "no decision" | terminal prompt at once |
| hook start | key missing or bad mode | KeyMissing | Y | instant "no decision", logged | terminal prompt |
| hook input | malformed JSON | HookInputInvalid | Y | instant "no decision", logged | terminal prompt |
| hook wait | no answer in 90 s | AnswerTimeout | Y | "no decision" + cancel | terminal prompt after 90 s (OV-1 open) |
| hook wait | wrong id or bad crypto | AnswerRejected | Y | ignore | nothing |
| daemon stream | drop, no Wi-Fi | StreamLost | Y | backoff 1 to 30 s, logged | status "Reconnecting (no Wi-Fi)" |
| daemon stream | TLS or clock | StreamTLS | Y | backoff, logged | status "Can't connect: check the clock" |
| daemon decrypt | junk or wrong key | DecryptFailed | Y | drop, logged | nothing |
| daemon parse | bad JSON, missing field, >4 KB | EnvelopeInvalid | Y | drop, logged | nothing |

### R3-1 — consistency (blocking)

**Problem**

> The revised Messages bullet removes every age check: the request no longer carries `ts` or `expires`, and a card lives for `ttl` seconds from when the daemon received it. The Success Criterion 'A message that fails to decrypt, is too old, or repeats an id never shows a card' still promises that old messages are rejected, and nothing in the design can now tell that a message is old. This is more than wording. The Brick's Wi-Fi is expected to drop on the train (Open Question 1), and the design does not say whether the daemon asks ntfy for missed messages when it reconnects (ntfy's `since` parameter returns cached messages). If it does, a request whose hook gave up minutes ago arrives as a fresh card with a full 90 seconds on it. Howard can then press A, see the card leave as answered, and believe he approved a command that the Mac has already sent back to its own prompt. The cancel that the hook posted would only clear the card if it is also replayed, and the design does not say so.

**Remedy**

> Choose one rule and make the Success Criterion match it. One option is that the daemon follows the topic for live messages only (no `since` backlog on reconnect), so anything sent during a drop is lost and the Mac times out, and then strike 'is too old' from the criterion. Another is to bound age by the relay's own receive time (ntfy stamps each message with `time`) compared against the relay time of the newest message the daemon has seen, which needs no Brick clock. In either case, say that a replayed cancel removes a replayed request before it is shown.

### R3-2 — clarity (minor)

**Problem**

> The card's lifetime starts when the daemon receives the request, while the hook's wait starts when the hook posts it. Relay and Wi-Fi delay (up to the 5 seconds the first Success Criterion allows, or more on a weak hotspot) therefore keep the card on screen after the hook has stopped waiting. An A press in that window looks accepted on the Brick but is discarded on the Mac. The hook's cancel normally removes the card, but only if it gets through.

**Remedy**

> Have the hook send a `ttl` a few seconds shorter than its real wait (for example, wait 90 s and send ttl 80), so a card always leaves the Brick before the hook stops listening.
